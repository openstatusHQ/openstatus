## Looking for an incident.io alternative?

**TL;DR:** Incident.io is an incident management platform with a status page attached. Openstatus is monitoring, incident management and status pages in one product. Incident.io is free until your team grows, then charges per user and still needs a separate monitoring tool. Openstatus is a flat price with unlimited team members, and the monitors that catch the outage are in the same workspace as the incident and the status page.

Many teams pick incident.io for its free plan: one status page, Slack incident response and single-team on-call for small teams. That works until the sixth engineer joins, you need a second status page, or someone asks how the status page knows something is down. The answer is that it doesn't: an incident.io status page changes when a person declares an incident, after an alert from a monitoring tool you run and pay for separately.

Openstatus covers the whole loop for teams that don't have a dedicated SRE team:

1. **Detect.** Monitors check your API from 28 regions in parallel. The alert lands in Slack before the first support ticket.
2. **Respond.** Declare the incident from the alert, Slack, the dashboard or an AI agent. It gets its own channel, a severity, a commander and one timeline.
3. **Communicate.** A linked status report updates your status page and notifies subscribers, worded for customers, separate from internal notes.
4. **Learn.** Once resolved, the agent drafts a blameless postmortem from the timeline, the Slack channel and the public updates.

## When to Choose openstatus

- You want **one product** for monitoring, incidents and the status page, without wiring three tools together
- Your team is growing and you don't want the bill to grow **per user**
- You need **more than one status page**, or private pages for customers and internal teams
- You want an **open-source** product you can inspect or self-host
- You work with **AI agents**, the CLI or Terraform, and want every action in the audit log

## When to Choose incident.io

- You need **on-call scheduling and escalation policies** built into the same tool
- You run incidents across **many teams and services** with a service catalog and custom workflows
- You already pay for monitoring (Datadog, Grafana) and only need the coordination layer
- You have a dedicated SRE or platform team and an enterprise budget

## Using openstatus alongside incident.io

Some teams keep incident.io for on-call and internal coordination and move monitoring and the public status page to openstatus:

- **Openstatus** checks your services from 28 regions and keeps your status page and subscribers informed
- **Incident.io** receives openstatus alerts through a webhook or PagerDuty and pages the person on call
- Your **customers** read the status page. Your **engineers** work in incident.io.

## Switching from incident.io

1. **Sign up** for a free openstatus account, no credit card required
2. **Add monitors** for the endpoints your incident.io alerts came from, or define them as code with the [CLI](/tooling/cli) or [Terraform](/tooling/terraform)
3. **Recreate your status page** components and point your custom domain to openstatus
4. **Connect Slack** with the [Slack agent](/docs/guides/how-to-setup-slack-agent) to declare and run incidents from your workspace
5. **Route alerts** to PagerDuty, Opsgenie or Grafana OnCall if you keep a separate on-call tool
